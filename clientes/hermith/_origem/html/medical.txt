Titanium for Medical Applications 
Why Titanium Matters in Medical Applications

Titanium has become one of the most important materials in the medical industry due to its biocompatibility, corrosion resistance, high strength and low weight. These properties make titanium suitable for medical applications where material reliability, long-term performance and compatibility with the human body are critical.

Medical Applications

Titanium is widely used in medical applications, including:

orthopaedic implants
spinal implants
trauma plates and screws
dental implants and prosthetics
surgical instruments
medical devices and components
tubes and small precision parts
components for medical equipment

Commercially pure titanium is often used where excellent biocompatibility and corrosion resistance are required. Titanium alloys, especially Grade 5 (Ti-6Al-4V) and Grade 23 (Ti-6Al-4V ELI), are commonly selected for applications that require higher strength, fatigue resistance and reliable mechanical performance.

Titanium Grades and Alloys

Common titanium grades used in medical applications include:

Commercially pure titanium Grades 1–4
Grade 5 (Ti-6Al-4V)
Grade 23 (Ti-6Al-4V ELI or Grade 5 ELI)
Ti-6Al-7Nb

Among these, Grade 23 is one of the key titanium alloys for medical applications. It is a version of Grade 5 with controlled lower levels of interstitial elements such as oxygen, carbon and iron, making it suitable for implants, prosthetics and other components where material reliability is important.
