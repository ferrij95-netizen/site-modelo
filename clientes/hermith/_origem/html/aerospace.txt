Titanium in Aerospace
Why Titanium Matters in Aerospace

Aerospace has been one of the key drivers of titanium alloy development for more than half a century, and it remains one of the most demanding environments for engineering materials.

Titanium is valued in aerospace applications due to its high strength-to-weight ratio, excellent corrosion resistance and reliable performance in demanding operating conditions. These properties help support lighter, more efficient and durable aircraft structures and components.

Titanium also offers good fatigue performance when correctly specified and designed, which is important for parts exposed to vibration, pressure cycles and long service life. In modern composite airframes, titanium is often preferred in contact areas because it can reduce galvanic corrosion concerns compared with other materials.

Aerospace Applications

Titanium is used across many critical areas of an aircraft, including:

structural airframe components
landing gear parts
engine components such as compressor blades, discs and casings
fasteners
hydraulic and pneumatic tubing
brackets and fittings throughout the fuselage and wings
composite-to-metal joining areas where corrosion compatibility is important
MRO replacement parts

Titanium is also widely used in maintenance, repair and overhaul (MRO) work, where replacement parts must meet the required material properties, specifications and certification requirements of the original components.
