Solutions

Nine fields of work for mining, dams and water bodies.

01

Tailings management and relocation

Tailings management and relocation at mining companies is an essential practice to ensure the safety, sustainability and environmental compliance of mining operations. It involves the efficient management of the solid waste generated during mineral processing, aiming to minimize environmental impacts and risks to nearby communities.

Learn more
02

Dam stability and decharacterization

Dam stability and decharacterization are essential for waste management and for the safety of neighboring communities. Decharacterization means the total or partial removal of the dam; stability is the structure’s ability to keep its integrity under adverse conditions.

Learn more
03

Tailings dewatering

Dewatering removes excess water from solid tailings and produces a drier, more stable material that is easier to handle, transport and dispose of. It reduces volume, lowers the risk of slides and collapses and allows the recovered water to be reused in industrial processes.

Learn more
04

Suction and discharge dredging

Suction and discharge dredging is a widely used method to remove sediment from water bodies such as rivers, lakes, ports and canals. A dredge fitted with a suction pump draws in solids and liquid slurry and carries them through pipelines to the designated areas.

Learn more
05

Precision dredging

Precision dredging is an advanced technique for removing sediment from water bodies, valued for its accuracy in sensitive or hard-to-reach areas, where excessive removal could harm wildlife, vegetation and water quality.

Learn more
06

Drying and dry stacking

Dry stacking of tailings is a waste management technique, used mainly in mining, that reduces the water in the tailings. Drier tailings are more stable, carry less risk of leaks and slides and are easier to dispose of.

Learn more
07

Mechanized conveyor belt cleaning

Conveyor belt cleaning is essential in mining for operational efficiency, accident prevention and product quality. Clean belts reduce material losses, cut component wear, avoid contamination and last longer, which lowers replacement and repair costs.

Learn more
08

Macrophyte removal

Removing macrophytes (aquatic plants), branches and logs from rivers, lakes and streams helps keep water quality and protects aquatic habitat. Debris blockages can raise flood risk during heavy rain, and removal also limits the buildup of sediment and nutrients.

Learn more
09

Mechanized removal

Mechanized removal plays a key role in mining and waste management, especially on rough or hard-to-reach ground, where long-reach removal allows material to be moved safely.

Learn more
